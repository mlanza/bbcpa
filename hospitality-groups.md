# Hospitality Groups

## Aim

Move from broad one-off church events toward repeatable, relational experiences where people can actually get to know each other.

The goal is not more events.
The goal is better conditions for friendship, trust, and discipleship.

## Problem We Are Solving

Open-to-all one-off events tend to create:

- high turnout but shallow connection
- social fatigue for hosts and attendees
- little continuity after the event
- lots of new faces, few lasting relationships

You can enjoy an evening with someone and still not know them.
If that happens once a year, relationship rarely forms.

## Strategy

Use **Planning Center Groups + Registrations** as a progression model:

1. **Discovery:** open special-interest group (Book Discussions, Sushi Outings, Board Games)
2. **Trial:** capped one-off outing (lower-commitment first step)
3. **Commitment:** short recurring series (same people, predictable rhythm)

Think: lobby, then table.
Not stadium, then disappear.

## Planning Center Model

### Groups (community layer)
Use Groups for:

- discoverability in Church Center
- joining an interest hub
- discussion and light communication
- ongoing identity (the group exists even between events)

### Registrations (logistics layer)
Use Registrations for outings that need tighter controls:

- hard capacity limits
- waitlist
- signup windows and deadlines
- custom details and forms
- multi-day events when needed

This split gives us both warmth and order:

- Groups = continuity
- Registrations = constraints

## Communication & Roles

- Hosts/leaders send official group messages.
- Members can participate in discussion where enabled.
- Not everyone should have mass-message permissions.

Practical outcome: the group works like an interest-based mailing list with conversation, while hosts guide cadence and clarity.

## Intimacy by Design (Default Settings)

Recommended defaults:

- event caps: **8–12 people**
- RSVP deadline: **clear and enforced**
- minimum threshold: define a number to run (e.g., 5)
- if threshold not met: host sends cancellation notice quickly
- post-event follow-up: invite engaged participants to short series
- series format: fixed cohort, monthly for 4–6 months

Capacity limits are not exclusion for its own sake.
They create room for people to be known.

## Example Flow (Board Games)

1. Launch **Board Games Interest Group** in Groups.
2. Let people join and discuss game preferences/times.
3. Post first outing using Registrations with cap = 10.
4. Run only if at least 6 register by deadline.
5. After event, invite engaged participants to a 6-month monthly table.
6. Keep that series cohort stable.

## Leadership Teaching Point

We are shifting from event production to relationship cultivation.

- Fewer large one-offs
- More small repeatable gatherings
- Clear pathways from curiosity to commitment

This approach lowers social noise and increases the chance of real connection.

## Pilot Recommendation

Start with one or two interests:

- Board Games
- Book Discussions

Run for one quarter, then evaluate:

- repeat attendance
- host energy/sustainability
- participant-reported sense of connection
- number of people moving from one-off to series

If the pilot yields depth, replicate the pattern across other interests.
