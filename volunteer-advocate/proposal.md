# Volunteer Advocate Proposal

This proposal defines the **Volunteer Advocate** role and keeps implementation intentionally small.

The role exists to advocate for volunteers so care is visible, credible, and consistent across ministries.

Goal: give leadership a small set of meaningful trends that protect people, pace initiatives, and support ministry decisions from reality.

At BBC, a faithful minority often carries most of the serving load while many others remain outside regular ministry participation. This creates a repeating cycle: leaders ask for help, someone steps in, and over time that person also becomes overextended.

The core issue is not a lack of goodwill but a credibility gap in the volunteer system. The problem is already present: people see shortages and overloaded volunteers as normal, and avoidance behaviors are already in play because visible guardrails and instituted care are largely absent. This points to a management gap as much as a willingness gap — how people are recruited, scheduled, supported, and retained over time.

## Congregational Signal

The proposal is a tool, not the answer by itself.

The answer is leaders regularly and visibly shepherding with care and attention — actively reviewing volunteer-capacity trends and showing how plans are being adjusted in response, just as financial leaders review financial trends and plan accordingly.

A real problem to address: some people avoid formal membership because they expect that saying yes once will make them a recurring resource with little regard for personal margin.

That expectation is understandable in a productivity culture, where people can be treated like capacity and ministry activity can be treated like success signals. Over time, classes launched and ministries staffed can matter more than whether people are actually known, cared for, and relationally connected.

When leadership consistently reports these trends and demonstrates capacity-aware planning, it sends a different message:
- people are not inventory,
- service should be sustainable,
- relational margin is a ministry value,
- and initiative pacing must follow real capacity.

Possible long-term signal (addendum):
- as capacity becomes healthier, leadership can encourage a culture where every attendee serves on just one team.

In short: the signal comes from visible shepherding behavior over time, with this proposal serving as the supporting structure.

## Metrics

Track only the minimum information needed to make better decisions.

Treat these like financials: track them as trends over time (year-over-year), not as one-off snapshots.

These numbers are meant to inform leadership judgment: when to pace initiatives, when to stabilize teams, and when to protect relational margin.

### 1) Participation Coverage

Track in the report period:
- average attendance (used as an upper-limit reference),
- number of members
- people involved in at least one ministry (for 6 or more months),
- people not currently involved in any ministry.

Use:
- show how much of the attending body is carrying ministry load,
- avoid planning as if all attenders are currently available capacity.

### 2) Ministry Capacity

Track per ministry:
- minimum slots required,
- target slots desired,
- slots filled.

Use:
- identify ministries below minimum,
- identify ministries at minimum but fragile,
- identify ministries at/near target.

### 3) Volunteer Capacity

Use a tiny pulse (annually) with two binary questions:
- **Overextended right now?** (Yes/No)
- **Do you expect to reduce your serving load in the next 6 months?** (Yes/No)

Use:
- detect present strain and near-term retention risk,
- reduce repeated pressure on the same reliable people,
- plan early for coverage transitions.

Trust guardrail:
- Capacity responses are for care and planning, not for identifying people for additional asks.

### 4) Ministry & Event Portfolio Mix (Intrinsic vs Instrumental)

Classify recurring offerings at a high level as primarily:
- **Instrumental** (task/mission/output), or
- **Intrinsic** (presence/relationship/care).

Use:
- reveal seasonal imbalance in what the church is offering,
- ensure task-heavy ministry does not crowd out relational ministry.

#### Why this matters

People need both forms of care:
- opportunities to serve meaningfully,
- opportunities to be known and supported apart from output.

Instrumental and intrinsic ministry create different kinds of pull:
- **Instrumental ministry** asks people to pour out for others. This is often Christ-like and necessary, but it usually requires recruiting and repeated asks because the pull comes from outside the person.
- **Intrinsic ministry** gives life while it gathers people. It tends to require less recruitment because the pull comes from within; people want to be there.

A healthy church needs both. If nearly everything is instrumental, leaders spend increasing effort to find resources and volunteers can feel consumed.

This classification is not about rating people or proving a theory. It is a practical check against drift toward all-output ministry.

### Target-First, Minimum-Safe Staffing Policy

Policy:
- Staff ministries to **target** whenever possible.
- If call-offs occur, allow coverage to run at **minimum** for that service period without emergency call-ins.
- Start replacement outreach only when coverage drops **below minimum** or a safety-critical role is affected.

Operational requirement:
- Each ministry maintains a one-screen **Reduced-Staffing Guide** that defines how it operates at minimum (what is simplified, deferred, or temporarily removed).
- **Minimum-safe is ministry-specific**: some ministries can flex down more easily, while others have tighter constraints.
- This policy does **not** normalize running at minimum as routine practice. It allows occasional operation at minimum when call-offs happen, to reduce unnecessary rescue-pressure.
