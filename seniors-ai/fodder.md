# Fodder

## Scenario: Personal Explainer
- We are all overloaded with information.
- I play board games. I can hand AI the rule book and ask specific rules questions as they come up.
- Same pattern for everyday documents: lawn mower manuals, warranties, legal documents, product paperwork.
- It can help a person navigate complexity when something is not easily understood.
- It can also help you find the needle in a haystack.
- Example: you have a few specific questions and don’t want to read the entire employee manual to find the answers.
- Upload the document to ChatGPT, ask plain questions, and get clear, capable answers fast.

## Scenario: Travel Agent
- This is a real-world job: understanding your preferences and constraints, then planning a trip that fits.
- The work is mostly gathering and processing information, then matching options to custom criteria.
- It can include flights, lodging, and excursions.
- You know how long this takes if you have ever tried to plan a trip yourself.
- This is exactly the kind of knowledge work AI will increasingly do.
- As more of that information is available through the internet, more of these roles become automatable.
- You might meet it at a self-service kiosk, or by calling an agency and speaking to a digital persona.
- It can sound human, ask good questions, understand your goals, and walk you through the process start to finish.

## Scenario: Data Entry Assistant
- Imagine a role where forms arrive both digitally and on paper through the mail.
- Paper forms can be scanned in, so both channels become digital inputs.
- AI can read the forms, extract the right facts, and enter the data into the main system.
- It can also check each submission against completion guidelines and required fields.
- If something is missing or incorrect, it can draft a custom letter and return the form with clear correction requests.
- Most traditional software rejects imperfect input.
- AI systems can be more resourceful: they can work around obstacles, resolve ambiguity, and make bounded judgment calls.

## Scenario: Scheduling Coordinator
- Imagine an AI that answers the phone and helps create, move, or cancel appointments.
- It can read the business calendar and negotiate which slot works best for you.
- Key change: it can understand normal human speech and interact with people in real time.
- Think of phone trees today: “Press 1 for hours, Press 2 to schedule, Press 3 for insurance.”
- That menu model can fade away.
- You just say what you need, and the AI routes you to the right person—or handles the request itself when it can.

## Scenario: Hotel Operations Coordinator
- At the core, this role tracks important events and incoming demands in real time.
- Events include checkouts, room readiness, maintenance alerts, and shift changes.
- Demands include room service, special requests, late arrivals, and guest complaints.
- It dispatches the right staff at the right time and reprioritizes as conditions change.
- It uses judgment, not just binary rules.
- It can weigh many factors at once: loyalty/VIP status, first-time guests, family needs, timing pressure, and service recovery risk.
- It can also follow spoken goals and guidance from a human manager.
- Example manager guidance: “Tonight we have a wedding party and several first-time guests checking in after long flights. Keep the bridal family on the same floor, prioritize quiet rooms for tired travelers, and if we run short, move upgrades from low-priority business travelers before touching accessible rooms.”
- Takeaway: this is not just booking rooms; it is continuous, policy-shaped decision-making across staff, constraints, and guest experience.

## A Real Story: “I thought they had my daughter.”
- Arizona mother Jennifer DeStefano gets a call from an unknown number.
- She hears what sounds exactly like her daughter crying: “Mom…”
- A man takes over, threatens harm, demands money.
- She later confirms her daughter is safe. It was a scam.
- Payoff: this is not theory; this is already happening.
- The scam used panic, urgency, and a familiar voice.
- Fear narrows judgment.
- AI can make fake feel personal.
- Easily extract the voice from social media account.
- Payoff: we need a better response pattern, not more panic.

## Set Aside from Prior Planning (AI onboarding/use-cases)
- Framing line: “You don’t have to become an expert. But everyone should know what this tool can do.”
- Goal framing: appetite, not pressure.

### Useful in 5 minutes (low learning curve)
- Draft or rewrite a text/message clearly and kindly.
- Summarize a long article into a few bullet points.
- Turn a recipe into a grocery list.
- Create questions before a doctor visit.
- Explain a confusing bill/letter in plain language.
- Plan a simple day trip with constraints.

### “Didn’t know it could do that” (possibility)
- Create a song from an idea/lyrics.
- Restore/colorize old family photos.
- Build Bible study or class discussion prompts.
- Turn rough thoughts into a testimony/teaching outline.
- Build a family history timeline from notes.

- Include non-creatives: AI helps with clarity, organization, and communication.
- Metaphor: AI as a calculator for language/planning/creativity.
- Positioning line: AI may be to this generation what the internet was to the last.
