# Seniors at Church — AI Talk Plan

## Goal
Help seniors understand what is changing in the world because of AI, with special focus on deception, scams, and practical safety habits.

## Core Message
- AI is becoming a **faux reality machine**.
- Seeing and hearing are no longer enough to trust something.
- The winning move is not panic; it is disciplined verification.

## Opening Hook (Faux Reality)
- Play ~90 seconds of an AI-generated worship song.
- Reveal plainly: “I made that with AI.”
- Pivot: if AI can convincingly generate songs, it can also generate fake voices, fake videos, fake documents, and fake identities.
- Landing line: **“Seeing and hearing are no longer believing.”**

## Talk Structure (3 Parts)

### Part 1 — What Is AI?
Purpose: remove mystique without minimizing risk.

- AI is not alive, not moral, not conscious.
- It is pattern-prediction software that can sound confident.
- Moral danger comes from people using it, shaping it, and deploying it.

#### Teaching frame: Mind, Body, Reach
- **Mind (Model):** pattern engine that generates likely next outputs.
- **Body (Harness):** software system that gives the model memory, workflow, and coordination.
- **Reach (Tools/Permissions):** what it can see and what it can change.

Key point: power and risk rise together as permissions expand.

### Part 2 — What Can It Do (Now and Soon)?
Purpose: show why this is a societal shift, not a niche app.

- AI can already perform significant digital knowledge work.
- It naturally lives in phones, kiosks, websites, and call systems.
- Businesses are redesigning software so AI can operate workflows once reserved for people.
- This creates real economic and social change: jobs reshaped, services automated, decisions accelerated.

#### Security implications
- Better phishing, cloned voices, fake authority, fake urgency.
- Deepfakes make emotional manipulation easier.
- New pattern: scammer may target your AI assistant, not just you.
- Practical stance: treat AI as powerful but gullible when given authority.

### Part 3 — Life on the Road Ahead
Purpose: orient people to how daily life changes, then take a brief safety detour.

#### Main thread (road ahead)
- Digital trust will get more expensive: verification becomes normal behavior.
- More services will be AI-mediated (phone, kiosks, websites, support desks).
- The practical skill to carry forward is discernment, not technical expertise.

#### Light detour: Scam survival rules
- Briefly pause the future-facing thread and give a compact safety protocol.
- Frame: “Since AI raises deception quality, here are 3 rules to keep you safe.”

##### Rule 1: Trust nothing you receive
- Do not trust inbound links, phone numbers, caller ID, QR codes, or attachments.
- If they sent it to you, treat it as untrusted.

##### Rule 2: Never react. Always initiate.
- End the inbound interaction.
- Reconnect using known numbers, official apps, or bookmarked sites.

##### Rule 3: Break contact when pressure appears
- Urgency, secrecy, and fear are control tactics.
- Hang up, verify independently, involve family, then act.

##### Emergency scam protocol (kidnapping/ransom style)
1. End call immediately.
2. Contact the loved one directly via known number.
3. Contact another trusted family member.
4. If unresolved, call police.
5. Preserve evidence; do not re-engage.

#### Return to main thread
- “That detour was about safety. Back to the big picture: life in an AI-shaped world will reward people who verify before they trust.”

## Tone Targets
- Calm, practical, grounded.
- Respect concern without feeding hysteria.
- Emphasize agency: you can’t stop all deception, but you can control your response pattern.

## Closing
- We are entering a world where digital appearances are cheap to fake.
- The durable skill is not technical mastery; it is discernment under pressure.
- Final line option: **“In this new world, verification is an act of wisdom.”**
