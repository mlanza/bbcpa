# Devotional Outline — Deacons

## Opening: Two Stories That Should Trouble Us

- Sunday's sermon — a man with same-sex attraction
  - He said: I will never be happy. Torn between Scripture and desire.
  - When I heard "It wasn't long afterwards that he came back..." — I anticipated the worst
  - What I got was the unexpected best
  - He studied Scripture deeply. Came back and said: "I love God more than my sexuality. This is right."
  - He moved toward God, not his desire

- Abraham — God's request to kill his beloved son
  - On the surface: this looks like a God no reasonable person could follow
  - Outsiders use it as evidence of a problematic God
  - But we trust his character — there was no unrighteousness in the request
  - The deeper reality was at work the whole time

- The common thread
  - Both stories ask for something that looks unreasonable from the outside
  - Both require trust before understanding
  - Both reward the person who leans in rather than walks away

## Transition

- God often makes demands of us we don't fully understand
- Jesus did the same — he taught in riddles and parables when he could have spoken plainly
- Why do you suppose he chose that?

## Jesus Teaches in Riddles — On Purpose

- Parables are not accidents of communication
- He teaches indirectly because the issue is not intelligence — it is willingness
- The heart is the root issue, not the mind

- The parable of the sower
  - We focus on the sower. Jesus wants us to look at the soils.
  - The question is not "Where is God sowing?" but "Which soil am I?"
  - Good soil: hears, understands, bears fruit
  - The posture required: inclining the heart toward understanding

- Why this method?
  - Because he is speaking to those whose hearts are inclined to lean in
  - He reveals truth to those willing to seek it
  - He conceals it from those who have already decided not to listen

## The Disposition of a Born-Again Person

- We are inclined to believe and trust God, whatever the cost
- We are charitable when something about the Bible doesn't make sense at face value
- We are not immediately put off kilter
- We expect something deeper must be at play
- His Word is trustworthy — that is our starting posture
- When things appear contradictory or morally challenging, we don't dismiss or blame God
- We go digging

## The Invitation: Notice When It's Happening to You

- The man with same-sex attraction illustrates the right posture
  - His desires pressed him one direction
  - He chose to lean into God's teaching, not away from it
  - He didn't bend the Word to suit the desire
- The tendency we all face
  - When God's teaching collides with where we want to go
  - We find a way to bring the Word into alignment with our desires
  - That is the normal drift
- A word on my own walk
  - I do not claim a blameless life — I sin like every other
  - What I have learned is: I don't bend the Word or dismiss things
  - I own my moral failings and apologize to God
  - I would rather name myself a sinner a thousand times over
    than find an interpretation that suits my desires
- The warning underneath
  - When the heart leans toward bending the Word
  - One is poised for deconstruction and falling away
  - The soil shifts under you

## Closing: The Soil Question

- Which soil am I?
- Do I bend the Word, or do I lean into it?
- Do I dismiss what confuses me, or do I go digging?
- Jesus does not want us to remain confused
  - He refuses to be understood on false terms
  - But for the one who returns with humility and patience
  - Confusion gives way to clarity
- The invitation is to be the good soil
  - Hear
  - Incline the heart
  - Seek understanding
  - Bear fruit
