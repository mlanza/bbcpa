# Why Jesus Wants People to Be Confused About Him

## Main Thesis

Jesus was not unclear by accident. His parables, riddles, and layered teaching were intentional. He taught in a way that invited true disciples to seek deeper understanding while exposing hearts that were unwilling to listen.

## Key Idea

Jesus does not hide truth from people who genuinely want it. He reveals truth in a way that tests the posture of the heart.

The issue is not usually intelligence. It is willingness.

## First-Century Jewish Context

Jesus taught as a Jewish rabbi, not as a modern Western preacher.

Rabbis often did not merely transfer information. They invited students into a journey of discovery. Students were expected to sit with a teaching, wrestle with it, and search the Scriptures for deeper meaning.

## The Four Levels of Interpretation: Pardes

The video explains four interpretive layers often associated with rabbinic reading:

### 1. Pashat: Plain Meaning

Pashat is the simple, surface-level meaning of the text.

This level matters because it anchors interpretation in context. Jesus never violates the plain meaning of Scripture, but the plain meaning is not always the final depth of the teaching.

### 2. Remez: Hint

Remez means “hint.”

Jesus often alludes to earlier Old Testament passages. These hints are like buried treasure, meant to send the listener back into Scripture.

The point is not to make Scripture easier, but to make the student spend more time with it.

### 3. Derash: Search or Inquiry

Derash involves making meaningful connections between passages of Scripture.

This is not random association. It is careful, contextual linking.

Example: Matthew 2:15 connects Jesus coming out of Egypt with Hosea 11:1, showing a pattern between God’s past actions with Israel and what God is now doing through Jesus.

### 4. Sod: Mystery or Revelation

Sod refers to understanding that comes by supernatural revelation from God.

Example: In Matthew 16, Peter confesses that Jesus is “the Christ, the Son of the living God.” Jesus says this was not revealed by flesh and blood, but by the Father in heaven.

## Why Jesus Used Parables

In Matthew 13, the disciples ask Jesus why He speaks in parables.

Jesus answers by quoting Isaiah: people hear but do not understand, see but do not perceive, because their hearts have grown dull.

The video emphasizes that Jesus identifies the heart as the root issue.

Parables reveal truth to those who are humble and hungry, while concealing it from those who have already decided not to listen.

## The Parable of the Sower

The video argues that the Parable of the Sower is often misunderstood.

The main focus is not the sower.

The soils are the key characters.

The question is not mainly, “Where are you sowing?”

The question is, “Which soil are you?”

## The Four Soils

### 1. The Path

This represents the person who hears the word of the kingdom but does not understand it. The evil one snatches away what was sown in the heart.

### 2. Rocky Ground

This represents the person who receives the word with joy but has no root. When tribulation or persecution comes, he falls away.

### 3. Thorny Ground

This represents the person who hears the word, but worldly cares and the deceitfulness of riches choke it, making it unfruitful.

### 4. Good Soil

This represents the person who hears the word, understands it, and bears fruit.

The good soil is the person with a heart inclined toward understanding.

## Connection to Proverbs 2

The video connects Matthew 13 with Proverbs 2.

Proverbs says to receive God’s words, treasure His commandments, make the ear attentive to wisdom, and incline the heart to understanding.

This supports the idea that biblical understanding requires seeking, searching, humility, and persistence.

Understanding is compared to searching for hidden treasure.

## Spiritual Posture Required

Jesus’ teaching invites a lifestyle of seeking God’s word.

This involves:

* Returning to Scripture again and again
* Inclining the heart toward understanding
* Accepting that clarity may require time
* Letting go of false expectations
* Approaching Jesus with humility

## Why Some Rejected Jesus

Many rejected Jesus not because the evidence was absent, but because accepting Him required them to surrender their expectations.

They thought they already understood Scripture. When Jesus did not fit their framework, they closed themselves off instead of returning to the text.

They proved themselves to be the wrong kind of soil.

## Parables Separate Two Kinds of People

Jesus’ parables separate:

Those who want easy answers from those who want truth.

Those who want confirmation from those who want transformation.

Those who are fans from those who are disciples.

## Final Takeaway

Jesus does not want people to remain confused forever. But He refuses to be understood on false terms.

If someone comes to Jesus demanding that He fit their expectations, He may remain confusing.

But if someone returns to God’s word with humility, patience, and a heart inclined to understand, confusion can give way to clarity.

And clarity bears fruit.

## Reflection Questions

3. Which soil best describes my current response to God’s word?
4. Where have my expectations made it harder for me to understand Jesus?
5. Am I cultivating a habit of inclining my heart toward understanding?
