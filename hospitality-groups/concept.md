# Hospitality Groups

## Aim

Move from broad one-off church events toward repeatable, relational experiences where people can actually get to know each other.

The goal is not more events.
The goal is better conditions for friendship, trust, and discipleship.

## Introduction Plan (Leadership First, Then Church-Wide)

### Phase 1: Leadership Brief
Start with a short leadership brief so ministry leaders understand:

- the purpose (relational connection, not event volume)
- the structure (Groups + optional capped events)
- the role flow (interested host → Hospitality Director support → launch)

### Phase 2: Church Introduction (5 minutes or less)
Give a short up-front explanation in service or gathering context:

- what Hospitality Groups are
- why they exist
- how to join
- where to learn more (link/QR to one page)

Keep it brief and practical.

### Ongoing Rhythm

- people with gathering ideas contact the **Hospitality Director** for setup support
- as new groups launch, announce them **once per quarter**
- quarterly mention is a reminder layer for people who do not check listings regularly

## Operating Model (Who Does What)

### Hospitality Director (owner)
Responsible for the lane overall:

- receive and review new group ideas
- vet ideas for fit, safety, and ministry value
- approve or decline launch requests
- onboard and authorize hosts
- set up initial group structure in Planning Center (or delegate setup)
- provide host guidance and SOPs
- monitor quality and consistency across groups

### Hosts (approved leaders)
Once approved and configured, hosts can:

- lead conversation and communication in their group
- propose and schedule hospitality events tied to that group
- run events with clear caps and deadlines
- follow host SOP for communication, follow-up, and cancellations

Host expectation: do not post events "just to fill a calendar."
Post when there is visible group interest.

### Members
Members can:

- join interest-based groups
- participate in discussion
- register for events as offered

## Permissions & Authorization (Planning Center)

This requires intentional role/permission management inside Planning Center.

- not everyone should be able to create hospitality groups
- not everyone should be able to create hospitality events
- Hospitality Director (or delegated admin) grants host-level access case by case

Practical policy:

1. person expresses interest in hosting
2. Hospitality Director vets the concept and the person
3. access is granted for that scope
4. host launches with SOP in hand

## Event Release Rule (Interest Before Event)

Hospitality events should generally be announced only after enough interest is visible in the group.

Use simple readiness checks such as:

- active member count in the group
- discussion signal (comments/replies)
- quick interest poll

Then publish the event with:

- clear cap
- deadline
- minimum threshold to run

## Communications Cadence

Two recurring communication layers:

1. **Initial launch communication** explaining the model to church and leadership.
2. **Quarterly new-group update** highlighting recently formed groups and where to join.

This keeps visibility high without constant announcement fatigue.

## Host Guidance Pack (Required)

Hospitality Director maintains a lightweight host packet covering:

- purpose and tone of Hospitality Groups
- who this serves (especially people missed by large-event patterns)
- event design defaults (size, cadence, thresholds)
- communication templates (invite, reminder, cancellation, follow-up)
- safety/pastoral escalation expectations
- Planning Center setup checklist

### Member Journey

1. Person joins one or more interest-based groups.
2. Interest and conversation build over time.
3. Hosts offer small, capped gatherings.
4. Gatherings prioritize relationship and shared bonds.

Framing: less “you serve or are served,” more “you know and are known.”

## Problem We Are Solving

Open-to-all one-off events tend to create:

- high turnout but shallow connection
- social fatigue for hosts and attendees
- little continuity after the event
- lots of new faces, few lasting relationships

People often default to existing cliques in large gatherings.
That is normal social behavior, but it can leave newer or quieter people on the edges.

You can enjoy an evening with someone and still not know them.
If that happens once a year, relationship rarely forms.

## Who This Especially Serves

This model intentionally serves people who are often overlooked by standard church social patterns:

- people who find large, one-off gatherings off-putting
- people who do not connect well through presentation-only formats (class, lecture, study as the only mode)
- people who connect better through smaller, repeat interactions

Hospitality Groups are not a replacement for classes, studies, or larger events.
They are a parallel lane for relational connection.

The goal is a **seasonal rhythm**: periodic bursts of activity with breaks in between.
In that rhythm, people see enough of the same faces to move from recognition to relationship.

## Energy Reality (Why This Matters)

Many faithful people are already serving hard.
They show up out of duty, conviction, and love for others.

But if most church activity feels "cup-emptying" for a certain kind of person, eventually relational life suffers.
By the time connection opportunities appear, people are often too spent to engage.

Hospitality Groups aim to create more "cup-filling" relational moments:

- lower social pressure
- smaller settings
- familiar faces over time
- enough repetition to build trust

This is not self-centered discipleship.
It is sustainable discipleship: people who are regularly known and encouraged are better able to keep serving others with joy.

## Why Groups Fit: Marketing, Not Selling

A core design choice here is **invitation over recruitment pressure**.

- **Selling** = person-by-person coaxing (“hey, you wanna…?”)
- **Marketing** = clear offer, visible to many, opt-in by interest

For hospitality, selling is usually a poor fit:

- it assumes interest that may not exist
- it pushes people into activities they would not naturally choose
- it can create polite attendance without genuine desire

Groups flip that script.
People self-select because they actually want the common bond.

That creates healthier conditions:

- lower pressure
- clearer interest signal
- better attendance quality
- greater chance of meaningful connection

The goal is not to persuade reluctant participants.
The goal is to gather willing participants.

This is one reason Hospitality Groups can be more cup-filling:
people are there by choice, not by coaxing.

## Strategy

Use **Planning Center Groups + Registrations** as a progression model:

1. **Discovery:** open special-interest group (Book Discussions, Sushi Outings, Board Games)
2. **Trial:** capped one-off outing (lower-commitment first step)
3. **Commitment:** short recurring series (same people, predictable rhythm)

Think: lobby, then table.
Not stadium, then disappear.

## Planning Center Reality Check (No Custom App Assumptions)

This proposal is about **Planning Center as it exists today**, not custom software.

- There is no built-in universal `is_hospitality` switch across all Planning Center products.
- So identification/filtering is a **manual process + SOP** decision at first.
- We should assume human consistency, not automation, until proven otherwise.

Practical implication for the “church-wide events page” question:

- If hospitality outings should stay off the main events page, that must be handled by how we publish/classify them inside Planning Center (and by staff process), not by a custom app flag.

## Calendar Separation Pattern (Recommended)

Use two calendar lanes in Planning Center:

- **Main Church Calendar** for broad church events
- **Hospitality Calendar** for smaller hospitality gatherings

Then publish them to different destinations:

- main events page shows Main Church Calendar
- hospitality page shows Hospitality Calendar

This gives site maintainers a clear filter boundary without custom development.

## Initial Manual SOP

When creating hospitality-related items in Planning Center:

1. Use a consistent naming marker (example: `Hospitality: Board Games Night`).
2. Place them in the agreed Planning Center category/page/location used for hospitality items.
3. Keep a short checklist for hosts/admins so this is done the same way every time.
4. Review monthly for drift (misclassified items, missed markers).

This keeps filtering possible through conventions now, and leaves room for tighter automation later if needed.

## Planning Center Model

### Groups (community layer)
Use Groups for:

- discoverability in Church Center
- joining an interest hub
- discussion and light communication
- ongoing identity (the group exists even between events)

### Registrations (logistics layer)
Use Registrations for outings that need tighter controls:

- hard capacity limits
- waitlist
- signup windows and deadlines
- custom details and forms
- multi-day events when needed

This split gives us both warmth and order:

- Groups = continuity
- Registrations = constraints

## Communication & Roles

- Hosts/leaders send official group messages.
- Members can participate in discussion where enabled.
- Not everyone should have mass-message permissions.

Practical outcome: the group works like an interest-based mailing list with conversation, while hosts guide cadence and clarity.

## Intimacy by Design (Default Settings)

Recommended defaults:

- event caps: **8–12 people**
- RSVP deadline: **clear and enforced**
- minimum threshold: define a number to run (e.g., 5)
- if threshold not met: host sends cancellation notice quickly
- post-event follow-up: invite engaged participants to short series
- series format: fixed cohort, monthly for 4–6 months

Capacity limits are not exclusion for its own sake.
They create room for people to be known.

## Example Flow (Board Games)

1. Launch **Board Games Interest Group** in Groups.
2. Let people join and discuss game preferences/times.
3. Post first outing using Registrations with cap = 10.
4. Run only if at least 6 register by deadline.
5. After event, invite engaged participants to a 6-month monthly table.
6. Keep that series cohort stable.

## Leadership Teaching Point

We are shifting from event production to relationship cultivation.

- Fewer large one-offs
- More small repeatable gatherings
- Clear pathways from curiosity to commitment

This approach lowers social noise and increases the chance of real connection.

## Pilot Recommendation

Start with one or two interests:

- Board Games
- Book Discussions

Run for one quarter, then evaluate:

- repeat attendance
- host energy/sustainability
- participant-reported sense of connection
- number of people moving from one-off to series

If the pilot yields depth, replicate the pattern across other interests.
