# Hospitality Groups Proposal

- **Church:** Bible Baptist Church
- **Author:** Mario T. Lanza
- **Date:** October 4, 2026
- **Audience:** Elders

Working draft for elder review and approval.

## Decision Requested

I am requesting approval to launch Hospitality Groups as a standing ministry initiative, with a formal 12-month review for effectiveness, sustainability, and fit.

This is designed as a low-overhead, distributed ministry: volunteer hosts carry most execution, while I maintain system operations and quality control through Planning Center.

## Purpose

We are strong at mobilizing people for ministry, but weaker at creating unhurried space for relationships to deepen.
As a result, people can be highly involved and still feel unknown.
Hospitality Groups are designed to address that gap by creating regular, low-overhead settings where people are valued for who they are, not only for what they do.
This initiative aims to strengthen the relational fabric of the church without increasing leadership load.

## Problem / Opportunity

Our church rightly values relational ministry, and many people are genuinely served by current church structures. At the same time, some people remain under-served by those same patterns, even when they are involved and present.

Current touchpoints often create warm but brief interaction. They are meaningful, but they do not always create the repeated, smaller-context rhythm needed for deeper connection. In larger or infrequent settings, people can participate consistently and still remain largely unknown.

The gap is not activity; the gap is intimacy. For some people, most existing pathways feel duty-heavy and cup-emptying rather than relationally renewing. Over time, this can produce relationships that remain thin and situational.

Hospitality Groups address this gap through interest-based, repeatable gatherings where people can know and be known over time. The opportunity is to create a complementary relational lane that strengthens church connection without replacing existing ministries and without increasing leadership burden.

## Proposed Solution

Hospitality Groups are interest-based relational groups designed to help people move from casual recognition to meaningful connection over time.

Shared interest is not a side detail in this model; it is part of the relational engine. People often bond more naturally when they are enjoying the same thing together, because conversation has a real center of gravity. In that sense, shared interest helps move relationships from polite interaction toward mutual knowing. As C.S. Lewis observes in *The Four Loves*, friendship often begins when people discover they are turned toward the same thing.

Just as important, the point of gathering is not the activity alone but relationship. Groups must have a relational buffer around the activity so people have real opportunity to know and be known.

The flow is intentionally simple. A potential host brings a group idea to the Hospitality Groups Director. The idea is vetted for fit and appropriateness, then the group is created in Planning Center and the host is onboarded.

From there, church members discover and join groups by interest through normal visibility channels and periodic reminders. As sufficient interest accumulates — and this allows for the possibility it could take a while — the host offers one or more "feeler" events.

Feeler events are low-stakes, try-before-commitment gatherings. Their purpose is not event volume but discernment and relational fit.

Based on how the feeler goes, registrants may choose whether to sign up for a fixed-session series that follows (as already described in the group vision). Series are intentionally expect between 4-8 sessions, depending on the activity. The series is the primary value proposition: repeated gatherings (weekly, biweekly, etc.) with familiar people, where deeper connection can form.

Series are intentionally seasonal. After a season ends, there are planned lulls or off-seasons before another season is offered. The design is occasional opportunity, not high-frequency obligation.

The events are driven by RSVPs (using Planning Center registrations), with attendance limits set by each host based on format, venue, and relational goals. Hosts may keep events intentionally small to foster deeper intimacy, choose a larger cap, or use no cap when appropriate.

Like a club model, groups may also define a clear demographic fit (for example couples, men, women, families, or shared life stage). This is not exclusion for its own sake; it is a way to improve relational fit and conversational depth.

This model does not replace existing ministries or recruit by pressure. It adds a low-overhead relational lane where people gather by desire, not obligation, and where intimacy has room to grow.

As a matter of policy, Hospitality Groups are not a lane for teaching the flock on biblical or moral issues. That lane remains separate and requires elder approval.

Illustrative Hospitality Group concepts are listed in Appendix A. Exploratory future service concepts are listed in Appendix B.

## Operating Model

### Lifecycle

This model has a clear lifecycle:

1. host launches group
1. allow time for group discovery and registrations and await critical mass
1. host feeler
1. host series

### Platform and Structure

Initial implementation uses Planning Center Groups and Planning Center Registrations.

A dedicated `Hospitality` category will be created under Planning Center Groups so this lane is clearly separated and filterable.

### Policy Boundary (Required)

Hospitality Groups are relational, not doctrinal-teaching groups.

- groups must not function as venues for teaching the flock on biblical or moral issues
- any proposed group that includes doctrinal or moral instruction is out of scope for this ministry lane
- if a proposal touches that lane, it must be referred for elder review and approval before proceeding

### Group Description Template (Required)

Each group must publish a clear description authored by the host. This creates expectation clarity before people register.

Required fields:

- purpose: why this group exists relationally
- target demographic: who the group is designed for (for example couples, men, women, families, life stage, or open)
- feeler format: what a feeler event is like and what attendees should expect
- series format: what a series session is like and what attendees should expect
- attendance limits: cap policy for events (intentionally small cap, larger cap, or no cap)

Note on group size: Hosts are encouraged to think intentionally about size. Smaller gatherings often foster stronger intimacy, but this proposal does not require a fixed cap across all groups.

### Pre-Launch Infrastructure (Required)

Before launch, the following infrastructure must be in place:

- a Hospitality Groups page where people can discover groups and sign up
- a Hospitality Events page where people can view and register for events
- clear navigation and visibility from normal church communication channels
- space on the Hospitality Groups page for vision, host onboarding guidance, and my contact information

I will provide the content (vision and onboarding materials). Web support is needed to provide and configure the pages.

### Hospitality Groups Director (My Role)

I serve as the primary admin and operational owner for this initiative.

I will:

- receive and vet host ideas for fit and appropriateness, including policy-boundary screening
- refer any proposal involving biblical/moral teaching to elders for review and approval before proceeding
- create Hospitality-category groups in Planning Center
- onboard and train hosts
- shepherd hosts over time
- maintain quality and consistency in the lane

### Host Role

Hosts own local execution within their approved group.

Hosts will:

- set and communicate group vision
- author and maintain the required group description template fields (purpose, target demographic, feeler format, series format, and attendance-limit policy)
- initiate conversation in their group
- create and run hospitality events using Registrations
- move from feelers to series only when enough interest is present
- set attendance caps that fit their format and relational goals (including intentionally small caps when desired)
- avoid framing or running the group as a venue for teaching biblical or moral issues (that requires elder-approved channels)

### Communications Support Role

Communications support is essential for viability, especially because this model depends on slow, organic interest over time.

Launch communications:

- initiative is introduced on a Sunday with purpose and distinctives
- a short spoken introduction (approximately 5 minutes) can be used

Ongoing communications (once per trimester):

- mention the Hospitality Groups page during announcements
- highlight newly launched groups
- reinforce where people can browse and join
- optionally echo in bulletin and other church communication channels

This is awareness-building, not pressure recruitment. The model is marketing-driven: making cup-filling opportunities visible so people can opt in by genuine interest.

### Reporting and Review

There is no heavy ongoing leadership management required in this model.

I will provide a list of newly launched groups each trimester for the announcement.

I will also provide an annual report to leadership on participation, movement from feelers to series, and overall fruit/sustainability. At that point, leadership can decide whether to continue as-is, adjust, or stop.

## Leadership Supports Required

To launch and sustain this ministry model, I am requesting the following leadership supports:

1. Approve launch of Hospitality Groups as a standing ministry initiative.
2. Assign web support to build and configure the Hospitality Groups page and Hospitality Events page before launch.
3. Assign communications support for launch messaging plus 3 annual reminder mentions (trimester cadence), using the brief I provide and including mention of newly launched groups.
4. Receive one annual report and provide continuation guidance (continue, adjust, or stop).

## Success Metrics

The success metric is number of **series** completed annually.

- **Healthy:** 5–6 completed series/year
- **Strong:** 8+ completed series/year

If healthy (allow for a half measure in year one) the ministry is working.

Secondary indicators (hosts, registrants, feelers) are useful leading signals, but they are not the main outcome.

Groups may remain quiet for long periods, or close without being treated as failure. Group turnover is expected in an organic, opt-in model.

Ministry-level concern is triggered only if it consistently fails to generate a healthy # of series.

Host burnout is not a risk in this design because the model is seasonal, low-pressure, and hosts may pause or close groups at will.

## Appendix B: Service Group Examples (Future Initiative)

**Lane Definition (Instrumental Value):** Service Groups exist to offer specific acts of help through host-initiated, seasonal opportunities. They are not intimacy-circle substitutes, though relationship can still grow through serving together.

These are exploratory ideas for a potential future initiative that applies a low-pressure, seasonal model to service opportunities.

This lane should only be considered after Hospitality Groups has demonstrated clear success. The current church baseline already contains many service-oriented pathways (serving or being served). Launching service groups too early risks reinforcing the same message: that a person's value is primarily tied to output. The priority of this proposal is to first establish a durable intrinsic-value lane where people are enjoyed, known, and connected apart from performance.

- **Bethesda Mission Meal Service**
- **Visitation**
- **Automobile Maintenance**
- **Yard Work**

Some service groups may use the same lifecycle (host launches group -> registrants -> feelers -> series), while others may remain limited seasonal offerings with a finite number of service slots rather than ongoing series.

This creates room for gift-based service groups. A host may offer a practical skill they enjoy (for example auto maintenance or yard work) as a limited, seasonal offering to church members at no charge.

This is a script flip: the church is not centrally initiating these service opportunities; the host is. Capacity is self-regulated by the host through seasonal limits, small cohorts, and clear start/stop windows. The design avoids open-ended demand and helps prevent burnout without adding heavy admin controls.

Expectation management is essential: registrants should understand that opportunities are seasonal, capacity-limited, and may fill before everyone receives a slot.

This creates a low-stakes way to test service ideas without pressure. People can register based on real interest, try a feeler, and then opt into a short seasonal series if there is fit. This can create a low-pressure way for people who are weary of formal ministry signups to dip a toe in the pool (feeler), then ease into the shallow end (series). It is often easier to sample a service opportunity with a defined end date.

A key added benefit is relational intention among participants themselves. In this model, serving is not only about the people being served; it is also about knowing and being known by the people serving beside you.

This distinction matters: Service Groups center instrumental value (a service output), while Hospitality Groups center intrinsic value (being known, enjoyed, and relationally formed). Both can be good, but they are not the same lane and should not be conflated.

Because this is future-facing, it is treated as optional and experimental rather than part of the initial launch scope.
